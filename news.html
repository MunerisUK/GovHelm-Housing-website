News

Releases, product decisions, audit results and licensing changes. Providers running GovHelm Housing receive all of this directly; this is the public record.

Release 4 September 2026

0.9 release candidate: homelessness casework

The 0.9 release candidate is available for testing. It brings the Homelessness Reduction Act duty sequence into the core case model: prevention, relief and main duty, with Personalised Housing Plans generated from case data and temporary accommodation placement and charging.

H-CLIC field capture now happens during normal casework rather than as a separate data entry exercise, with gap reporting so a return can be checked before the quarter closes. Providers evaluating against their own configuration are asked to report discrepancies against their current return.

Product 19 August 2026

Temporal households, and why they matter on review

Households are now versioned rather than mutated, so an assessment made two years ago can be reconstructed exactly as it stood, including who was in the household and what evidence was held at the time.

This is the change that makes historic banding decisions defensible on review. It lands in 0.9, with a documented migration path for providers already on 0.8.

Accessibility 30 July 2026

Independent accessibility audit: results issued

An independent audit of the citizen portal and staff workspace against WCAG 2.2 AA has been completed. The full report, including every issue found, goes to providers evaluating or running the platform.

Eleven issues were identified, of which three were level A. All three are fixed in the 0.9 candidate; the remainder are tracked with target releases. Sharing the failures as well as the passes is deliberate: an accessibility claim nobody can check is not worth much.

Customers 8 July 2026

Advisory group sets the 1.1 priorities

The customer advisory group met to work through what follows 1.0. Asset and compliance data came first by some distance, driven by the reporting burden the Social Housing (Regulation) Act has placed on providers.

The group also asked for local lettings plans to be configurable without support, which has generated more questions than any other part of the scheme engine.

Release 17 June 2026

0.8: allocations, lettings and the citizen portal

0.8 completes the first end-to-end journey: an applicant can apply online, be assessed against the configured scheme, see their band with an explanation, bid on adverts and receive an offer, without any part of it being rekeyed by an officer.

The citizen portal ships at WCAG 2.2 AA with published test evidence. Choice-based lettings includes advert cycles, bidding by web, phone and text, and shortlisting that records which rule placed each entry.

Licensing 21 May 2026

Statutory changes will not be charged for

We have written into the licence terms that changes required by legislation or by a national return specification are delivered under the licence, not quoted as change requests. Every provider has to make those changes; charging separately for work none of them can decline is indefensible.

Also confirmed: six months' notice for any breaking API change from 1.0 onwards, and non-production environments are not separately licensed.

Security 2 April 2026

First independent penetration test

An independent penetration test against a reference deployment has been completed. Two medium findings and four low findings were identified; all six are fixed and the summary goes to providers on request.

The scope template is supplied so providers can commission equivalent testing against their own deployment, which is what actually matters for assurance.

Release 12 February 2026

0.7: the core platform and housing register

The first public release. 0.7 establishes the foundations: the party and household model, a property register keyed on UPRN, the case and workflow engine, permissions and the audit trail, with applications and needs assessment on top.

It is not production software and does not pretend to be. It is enough to evaluate the shape of the platform, run your own scheme through the rules engine, and tell us where we have got the housing domain wrong.

Stay informed

Three ways to follow the product

  • Release notes. Issued to every licensed provider with the full change list, migration steps and a security section.
  • Security advisories. Sent directly to providers running a deployment, after coordinated disclosure. Ask to be added to the list.
  • Advisory group. Quarterly, for providers running GovHelm Housing, with the roadmap reviewed before it is published.
