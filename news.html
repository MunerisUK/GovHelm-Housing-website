News

Releases, accepted design decisions, audit results and community updates. Everything here is also in the repository; this is simply the readable version.

Release 4 September 2026

0.9 release candidate: homelessness casework

The 0.9 release candidate is available for testing. It brings the Homelessness Reduction Act duty sequence into the core case model: prevention, relief and main duty, with Personalised Housing Plans generated from case data and temporary accommodation placement and charging.

H-CLIC field capture now happens during normal casework rather than as a separate data entry exercise, with gap reporting so a return can be checked before the quarter closes. Providers testing against their own configuration are asked to report discrepancies against their current return.

Design note 19 August 2026

Accepted: temporal households

The steering group has accepted the design note on temporal household composition. Households are now versioned rather than mutated, so an assessment made two years ago can be reconstructed exactly as it stood, including who was in the household and what evidence was held.

This is the change that makes historic banding decisions defensible on review. The note includes the migration path for existing deployments and lands in 0.9.

Accessibility 30 July 2026

Independent accessibility audit: results published

An independent audit of the citizen portal and staff workspace against WCAG 2.2 AA has been completed and published in full, including the issues found.

Eleven issues were identified, of which three were level A. All three are fixed in the 0.9 candidate; the remainder are tracked with target releases. Publishing the failures as well as the passes is deliberate: an accessibility claim nobody can check is not worth much.

Community 8 July 2026

Practitioner calls move to monthly

Attendance has grown enough that the practitioner call is moving from quarterly to monthly, on the second Tuesday. The format stays the same: an agenda published in advance, one substantive topic, and notes published afterwards.

Next month's topic is local lettings plans and how providers configure them, which has generated more discussion than any other configuration question this year.

Release 17 June 2026

0.8: allocations, lettings and the citizen portal

0.8 completes the first end-to-end journey: an applicant can apply online, be assessed against the configured scheme, see their band with an explanation, bid on adverts and receive an offer, without any part of it being rekeyed by an officer.

The citizen portal ships at WCAG 2.2 AA with published test evidence. Choice-based lettings includes advert cycles, bidding by web, phone and text, and shortlisting that records which rule placed each entry.

Project 21 May 2026

Technical steering group constituted

The technical steering group has met for the first time, with seats held by the maintainer team and by the organisations piloting IHAP. Minutes and decisions are published in the repository within five working days of each meeting.

Its first decisions: adopt the design note process for anything touching the data model or public API, and commit to six months' notice for breaking API changes from 1.0 onwards.

Security 2 April 2026

First independent penetration test

An independent penetration test against a reference deployment has been completed. Two medium findings and four low findings were identified; all six are fixed and the advisories are published.

The test scope template is in the repository so providers can commission equivalent testing against their own deployment, which is what actually matters for assurance.

Release 12 February 2026

0.7: the core platform and housing register

The first public release. 0.7 establishes the foundations: the party and household model, a property register keyed on UPRN, the case and workflow engine, permissions and the audit trail, with applications and needs assessment on top.

It is not production software and does not pretend to be. It is enough to evaluate the shape of the platform, run a scheme through the rules engine, and tell us where we have got the housing domain wrong.

Stay informed

Three ways to follow the project

  • Releases. Watch the repository for release notifications, which include the full change list, migration steps and security section.
  • Security advisories. Subscribe separately if you run a deployment. Advisories are published after coordinated disclosure.
  • Practitioner calls. Monthly, open to anyone, with notes published afterwards. See the community page.
