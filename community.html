Ways to help that are not code

Most of what the project needs is not programming

  • Test a release candidate against your real scheme and tell us what broke
  • Write up how your organisation configured something
  • Review resident-facing content for plain English
  • Contribute a translation or review an existing one
  • Report an accessibility barrier you hit
  • Improve the documentation you found confusing
  • Share a data mapping from an incumbent system
  • Comment on a design note from a practitioner's point of view
  • Present what you learned at a practitioner call

Especially valuable

Housing practitioners who are willing to read a design note and say “that is not how the duty actually works” are the single most useful contributors to this project. Developers can write code; they cannot invent the legislative detail, and getting it wrong is expensive to unpick later.

The same applies to accessibility. A barrier reported by someone who hit it in real use is worth more than any automated scan.

If you work in a housing team and want to help but do not know where to start, come to a practitioner call and listen. That is a legitimate contribution.

Code of conduct

The short version

The project follows the Contributor Covenant. In practice: assume good faith, criticise the work rather than the person, and remember that many contributors are doing this alongside a full-time job in a service under pressure.

Housing is a subject where people have strong and well-founded views, often shaped by difficult experience. Disagreement about policy, scheme design or priorities is expected and welcome. Personal attacks, harassment and dismissiveness towards people's lived experience of housing are not.

Reports of unacceptable behaviour go to the maintainer team and are handled privately. The full text is in CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md in the repository.
