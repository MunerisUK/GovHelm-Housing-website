About

A platform the housing sector can own

IHAP began with a simple observation: every housing provider in the country solves the same problems, separately, at considerable expense, and none of them get to keep what they paid for.

What IHAP is

IHAP — the Integrated Housing Application Platform — is a free and open source system for managing housing services end to end: the housing register, needs assessment, allocations and lettings, homelessness casework, tenancy management and the statutory returns that follow from all of it.

It is delivered as twelve modules over a shared core. You can run all of them, or run the register and allocations alongside systems you already have. The core is what makes the difference: one household record, one set of scheme rules, one audit trail, one permission model.

What IHAP is not

It is not a product with an open source marketing edition. There is no feature held back for a paid tier, no licence key and no per-seat count. The repository contains the whole platform.

It is also not a finance system, an asset management system or a contact centre. Those exist, most providers already run them, and IHAP's job is to connect to them cleanly rather than replace them.

Why it exists

Housing management software is a concentrated market. Contracts run for years, migration is expensive enough to be a deterrent in itself, and the cost of a change is set by the supplier rather than the difficulty of the work. Meanwhile the statutory landscape keeps moving: the Homelessness Reduction Act, the Social Housing (Regulation) Act, Awaab's Law, tenant satisfaction measures.

Each of those changes is implemented separately by every provider, paid for separately, and tested separately. Open source is the obvious answer to work that everybody has to do and nobody benefits from doing alone.

How it is built

The platform is a set of services around a PostgreSQL data store, packaged as containers, with a documented REST API and an event stream. It runs on a single virtual machine for a small provider and on Kubernetes for a large one. The architecture page covers this properly.

Development happens in the open. Issues, design notes, decisions and release planning are all in the repository, and the governance model sets out who decides what.

Design principles

Six commitments we hold the code to

One record for a household

If a person appears twice, something has gone wrong. Identity, relationships and address history are modelled once and shared by every module.

Configuration over code

Allocation schemes, bands, letters and workflows are data. Changing your scheme should not require a developer or a release.

The audit trail is a feature

Every decision records who, when, under which scheme version, and why. A decision you cannot explain is a decision you cannot defend.

Accessible or it is not finished

WCAG 2.2 AA is a merge requirement, not a later phase. The people most dependent on housing services are the least able to fight a bad interface.

Boring, documented technology

PostgreSQL, containers, REST, OpenAPI. A platform that outlives its original team has to be deployable by people who never met them.

Data belongs to the provider

Open schema, open formats, documented exports. Leaving should be as straightforward as arriving.

Stewardship

Who looks after the project

IHAP is stewarded by Muneris, which contributes the core engineering team, and directed by a technical steering group drawn from the organisations running the platform. Steering group decisions, including anything that changes the roadmap or the data model, are recorded as public design notes in the repository.

Contributions are welcome from anyone: providers, suppliers, individual developers, and residents who find something wrong with the portal. The contributing guide explains the process and the governance page explains how a contribution becomes a commitment.

Commercial support, hosting and implementation services are available from Muneris and from any other supplier who wants to offer them. The project does not certify or endorse suppliers, and no supplier gets privileged access to the roadmap.
