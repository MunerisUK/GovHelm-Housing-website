Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up in evaluation, procurement and information governance boards, answered as plainly as we can manage — including the ones about price.

The product

Is IHAP open source?

No. IHAP is commercial software under a licence fee. We say that plainly because the alternative — hinting at openness and charging for the parts that matter — wastes everybody's evaluation time.

What we have taken from the open source world is the parts that benefit you: a documented data model, configuration you control without raising a ticket, full exports, and a published API rather than an integration priced per connector.

Who builds and supports it?

Muneris. The same team writes the platform, runs the implementations and answers the support calls. See about.

Is it production ready?

Not yet. IHAP is pre-release and working towards 1.0, which is the first generally available release with a supported upgrade path and a stable API contract. The roadmap shows what has shipped and what has not.

Providers are running earlier versions in pilot and test environments. Going to production before 1.0 is possible but should be a deliberate, supported decision.

What does the licence actually cover?

The whole platform: every module, the API, the integration adapters, the migration and reconciliation tooling, the documentation set, platform updates and security patches. Non-production environments are not separately licensed.

Priced options alongside it are named support with response targets, out-of-hours cover, managed hosting, and implementation services.

Cost and procurement

What does it cost?

An annual subscription that scales with the size of the provider rather than with the number of users, so putting more of your team on the system never costs more. It is deliberately set well below what integrated housing management normally costs.

We will give you a figure for your size of organisation on the first call, before you have invested any time. Ask for pricing.

Why is it so much cheaper than the incumbents?

Three reasons, none of them a trick. The platform is one product rather than a set of acquired systems held together by interfaces. Configuration is genuinely configuration, so the work providers pay for repeatedly elsewhere is not chargeable work here. And we are not recovering the cost of an acquisition.

The honest caveat: low licence cost does not make implementation free. Migration, configuration and training are real work, and we would rather quote them properly than bury them.

Where does the real cost of a housing system sit?

Rarely in the licence. It sits in change control over the following five years, in the integration work that gets quoted per connector, and in the staff time lost to rekeying between systems that do not talk to each other. Those are the lines IHAP is designed to reduce.

Do we still need to run a procurement?

That is for your own standing orders to decide, and the answer usually depends on contract value and your thresholds. Low licence cost often brings the total below the threshold that would otherwise require a full tender, which is worth checking early with your procurement team.

What is the exit position?

Documented schema, standard formats and full export tooling included in the licence — not sold back to you at the point you want to leave. Extraction is not a chargeable event.

Technical

Can it run alongside our existing systems?

Yes, and most implementations do. The integrations hub is designed for a long transition period where IHAP owns some services and incumbent systems own others. See integrations.

Does it have to be hosted by you?

No. Run it on your own infrastructure, in your own cloud tenancy, or let us host it. The same container images run in all three cases, and the platform does not phone home. See deployment.

Can we see the source code?

Not as a general licence, but your security team can review the authentication path, the permission resolver and the audit implementation under non-disclosure, and source code escrow is available. See security.

How do we migrate five years of case notes?

With the migration tooling included in the licence, and with realistic expectations. Structured data maps cleanly; free-text notes are loaded with their provenance rather than parsed into false precision. Profiling the source data early is the single best predictor of how the migration goes.

Does it support Welsh legislation?

Partly. The core model covers England and Wales, and Welsh-specific duty sequences are in progress. Scotland and Northern Ireland are not currently in scope.

Risk and assurance

What happens if Muneris stops trading?

A fair question to ask any supplier, and the reason source code escrow is part of the standard arrangement rather than an upsell. Your data is in a documented PostgreSQL schema you can read without us, and the export tooling is already in your hands.

Who is liable if something goes wrong?

Liability sits where the contract puts it, and we would rather discuss that with your legal team than characterise it in marketing copy. The support and warranty terms are part of the licence agreement, not a separate negotiation.

How do we know a statutory change will be implemented?

Statutory changes affecting every provider are the highest priority on the roadmap and are delivered under the licence rather than quoted for. That is a deliberate commercial choice: charging providers separately for a change every one of them is legally required to make is indefensible.

Can we influence the roadmap?

Yes. Providers running IHAP sit on a customer advisory group that meets quarterly and sees the roadmap before it is published. Funding a specific piece of work brings it forward, but it does not buy exclusivity. See roadmap.

Will our information governance team be satisfied?

The security page sets out the controls, the threat model and the assurance evidence we provide, including penetration test summaries, a software bill of materials and code review under non-disclosure. Those are the things an IG review actually asks for.

Question not answered?

Ask it before you invest evaluation time, not after. We would rather tell you IHAP is a poor fit early than waste six weeks of your team's time.
