How IHAP compares

An honest comparison is worth more to you than a flattering one, so this page starts by admitting what IHAP has not done yet. Every capability claim below says whether it is deployed, built or only designed.

What is actually builtWhere the incumbents win

How to read this page

Three words, used precisely

Software comparisons usually collapse a roadmap and a running service into the same tick. These three markers are used throughout this site so that they cannot be confused.

Deployed

Running in a live service, handling real cases for real residents.

Built

Code complete and in a released version. You can run it today and test it.

Designed

Specified, scheduled and reviewed, but not yet built.

IHAP has no production deployments yet

This is the most important line on the page, so it is not buried in a footnote. IHAP is pre-release. Providers are running it in pilot and evaluation environments; nobody is yet running a live housing service on it.

Every established supplier can say the opposite, and that is a real advantage they hold over us. If proven production scale is your primary criterion today, you should weight it accordingly and we will not argue.

Why show you this

A comparison table that gave IHAP a tick in every row would be worthless, and you would find out which ticks were real during implementation. Six of the rows above are designed rather than built, and two are deliberately out of scope.

The roadmap carries the dates. They are intentions, not contractual commitments.

What you are probably comparing us against

Four archetypes, not four companies

Named vendors are deliberately absent. A comparison written by a supplier about its competitors is the least reliable document in any procurement, and we would rather describe the shapes honestly than characterise a named product we do not run.

Type A

The established integrated suite

The incumbent most authorities and large associations already run. Housing management, assets, income and often finance in one estate, usually assembled over two decades of acquisition and integration.

Where it is strong

  • Decades of production deployments at every scale
  • Breadth well beyond housing management
  • Known to your staff, your integrators and your framework

Where it tends to hurt

  • Priced per module and commonly per seat
  • Change delivered through change control, at a day rate
  • Integration often quoted per connector
  • Modules can behave like the separate products they once were

Type B

The modular specialist

Strong in one domain — typically assets and repairs, or income and arrears — and extended outwards into housing management from there.

Where it is strong

  • Genuine depth in the domain it started in
  • Often the best answer if that domain is your problem
  • Smaller, more responsive supplier relationship

Where it tends to hurt

  • Register, allocations and homelessness are usually the thinner end
  • You may still need a second system for the statutory casework
  • Data model shaped by the original domain, not by the household

Type C

The homelessness point solution

Built specifically for housing options and statutory homelessness casework, and usually very good at it.

Where it is strong

  • Deep Homelessness Reduction Act casework, deployed and proven
  • H-CLIC handled as a first-class concern
  • Quick to deploy because the scope is narrow

Where it tends to hurt

  • No housing register, allocations, lettings or tenancy
  • Applicant identity lives separately from your register
  • Another integration to build, run and reconcile

Type D

The configurable case-management platform

A general low-code or case-management platform configured for housing, usually by a systems integrator.

Where it is strong

  • Extremely flexible, and often already in your organisation
  • Corporate-wide licence may already be paid for
  • Configuration can move fast in the right hands

Where it tends to hurt

  • Housing and statutory logic is bespoke to your instance
  • You or your integrator own that logic forever, including legislative change
  • Statutory returns are usually built from scratch
  • Nobody else's fixes reach you

Where the incumbents are genuinely stronger

The case against choosing IHAP today

If this section did not exist you should distrust the rest of the page.

Production track record

They have run live housing services for decades, through every statutory change, at every scale. IHAP has not run one. No amount of engineering quality substitutes for that, and it is the single strongest argument against us.

Breadth beyond housing

A suite that also carries finance, assets and works management can be the right answer for an organisation that wants one supplier and one throat to choke. IHAP deliberately does less.

People who already know it

Your staff are trained, your integrators are experienced and the contractor market understands the product. Every migration spends that capital.

Proven at scale

Performance and resilience at 60,000 units is demonstrated rather than modelled. Our sizing guidance is engineering judgement, and we say so on the deployment page.

Procurement familiarity

Already on frameworks, already through your assurance process, with reference sites you can visit. We are a newer conversation for your procurement and IG teams.

Supplier longevity

A large incumbent is unlikely to disappear. We offer escrow and a documented schema precisely because that question is fair to ask of a smaller supplier.

How to test any of this

Questions that separate claims from capability

Ask every supplier on your list the same questions, including us. The answers are more revealing than any comparison table a vendor writes, this one included.

  1. Show me this running, today, with my scheme loaded. Not a slide, not a video. A configured environment separates built from designed faster than anything else.
  2. Which of these is in a release I can install this week, and which is on the roadmap? Ask for it in writing, per capability.
  3. Name three live sites of my size and let me speak to them unaccompanied.
  4. What did the last statutory change cost your existing customers? The answer tells you how the next one will be handled.
  5. Change a banding rule in front of me. If it needs a developer, it is code, not configuration, whatever the brochure says.
  6. Quote me the extraction cost at the end of the contract. Ask before you sign, not at renewal.
  7. Show me your accessibility test evidence, including the failures.

Our answers

Questions 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 we can answer now, and the evaluation environment answers most of them without us in the room.

Question 3 we cannot answer yet, because there are no live sites. Question 4 we have no history to point to. Those are honest gaps, not evasions.
